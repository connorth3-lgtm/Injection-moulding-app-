# NZQA provider shortlist — 2026

Status: **prospective provider-selection evidence only**. Checked 2026-09-15. No organisation listed here is recorded as having agreed to deliver, assess, moderate, endorse, own, accredit or seek NZQA approval for MouldMaster Academy.

Purpose: identify the strongest current organisations to approach for provider ownership or recognised-assessment support, using current NZQA/TEC evidence rather than assuming that public training marketing equals current consent, delivery or future ownership.

## Decision rule

A provider candidate is not promoted because it appears in marketing material alone. Prefer candidates where current evidence shows one or more of:

- NZQA-listed consent to assess in **Plastics Processing Technology** at levels covering the selected Injection Moulding standards;
- current or approved plastics/manufacturing programme capability;
- credible work-based-learning or employer-partnership capability;
- organisational continuity through the 2026–2027 VET transition.

Every candidate remains prospective until written confirmation is retained.

## 1. Ara Institute of Canterbury — strongest durable provider candidate found so far

**Current evidence**

- NZQA provider results show Ara Institute of Canterbury with consent to assess **Plastics Processing Technology to level 6** at multiple sites.
- A current NZQA Injection Moulding result page also shows Ara sites with Plastics Processing Technology consent to level 6.
- Ara returned to operation as a stand-alone polytechnic from **1 January 2026**.
- Ara is actively expanding employer-linked manufacturing delivery in 2026, including a new industry-led manufacturing initiative with Canterbury manufacturers.

**Why this matters for MouldMaster**

The selected MouldMaster standards are Levels 1–4, so Ara's current sub-field consent level is broad enough in level terms to make it a credible organisation to ask about exact Injection Moulding standard coverage, assessment ownership and future delivery. This does **not** establish that Ara currently assesses every selected standard, currently delivers an injection-moulding programme, or would accept a MouldMaster partnership.

**Questions to verify with Ara**

1. Does Ara's current Plastics Processing Technology consent cover standards 252, 255, 27926, 29515, 260 and 9713 without exclusions?
2. Is Ara currently assessing any Injection Moulding standards or plastics qualifications in 2026?
3. Would Ara consider acting as a future accredited provider/application owner for a provider-owned micro-credential or standards-based pathway?
4. Can Ara support workplace assessment nationally or only within defined regions/programmes?
5. What assessor, moderation, workplace-observation and learner-support requirements would Ara require before accepting externally developed learning resources?

**Current classification:** `provider-candidate-high-priority / external-confirmation-required`

## 2. Manufacturing & Engineering Industry Skills Board / Competenz — strongest current pathway and transition source

**Current evidence**

- NZQA lists the Manufacturing & Engineering Industry Skills Board with consent to assess **Plastics Processing Technology to level 6**.
- NZQA also lists approved plastics programmes under the organisation, including a Level 3 plastics-processing programme with Injection Moulding specialisation and Level 4 plastics-engineering/apprenticeship programmes.
- NZQA explicitly cautions that an organisation may not currently deliver every approved programme shown in its record.
- TEC states that Competenz is the current work-based-learning division of the Manufacturing & Engineering Industry Skills Board for manufacturing and engineering.
- TEC states that all ISB work-based-learning divisions must transition out of ISBs before the end of 2027.

**Why this matters for MouldMaster**

This is the strongest source for current standards, operational plastics training, workplace assessment practice and the transition destination for existing work-based provision. It is **not yet a safe durable provider-owner assumption** because the current Competenz work-based-learning function must move to a provider before the end of 2027.

**Questions to verify**

1. Which organisation will receive plastics/injection-moulding work-based-learning provision after the current transition?
2. Which of the selected standards are actively assessed now?
3. Which programme/qualification references and versions underpin current public Level 3 and Level 4 plastics pathways?
4. Would the future receiving provider be open to reviewing MouldMaster as formative curriculum support?

**Current classification:** `transition-authority-and-current-delivery-source / durable-owner-unresolved`

## 3. Te Pūkenga - EarnLearn records — verification candidate only, not primary plastics owner

**Current evidence**

- NZQA provider-result pages still show Te Pūkenga - EarnLearn sites with **Plastics Processing Technology to level 6** in some historical/current provider records.
- Current TEC information, however, places EarnLearn under the **Energy and Infrastructure ISB** with broad coverage focused on plumbing, gasfitting, drainlaying and electrical.
- EarnLearn's current public contact/training pages likewise focus on those trades rather than plastics.

**Decision**

Do not treat EarnLearn as a current plastics delivery candidate without direct confirmation. The NZQA consent record is useful evidence that capability/consent has existed in the system, but present organisational scope appears to have shifted.

**Current classification:** `verification-only / not-primary-shortlist`

## 4. Provider shortlist order

1. **Ara Institute of Canterbury** — strongest durable provider candidate currently visible from public evidence.
2. **MAEISB / Competenz** — first for current standards, assessment practice and transition destination; may identify the eventual provider owner.
3. **Other future receiving provider named through the 2026–2027 transition** — promote only when MAEISB/TEC or the provider confirms the transfer.
4. **EarnLearn or other legacy-consent holders** — investigate only if current plastics scope is confirmed.

## 5. Evidence still required before selection

Do not mark `G1-provider` complete until a named eligible provider supplies written evidence covering at least:

- willingness to explore provider/application ownership;
- current NZQA registration/accreditation status appropriate to the proposed route;
- exact consent-to-assess scope if DASS standards will be used;
- delivery model and geographic/workplace reach;
- assessor capability and moderation arrangements;
- learner-support, records, appeals and identity controls;
- ability to support workplace evidence where required;
- position on micro-credential vs standards-based pathway;
- transition continuity beyond 2027 where applicable.

## 6. Claim boundary

A provider's current consent to assess, approved programme record, meeting, reply, review, or willingness to explore the project does **not** mean MouldMaster is NZQA approved, accredited, nationally moderated, able to award credits, or evidence of workplace competence.

The shortlist is a provider-selection aid only.
