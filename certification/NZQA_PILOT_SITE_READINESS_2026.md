# NZQA workplace pilot-site readiness — 2026

Status: **pre-pilot checklist only**. No site is approved, selected or committed by this document.

## Purpose

Define the minimum conditions that must exist before MouldMaster is used in a real workplace learning or evidence pilot. The goal is to prevent a pilot from becoming uncontrolled machine instruction, unconsented data collection, or pseudo-assessment.

## Required roles before pilot start

The following roles must be explicitly identified where applicable:

- employer/site owner;
- learner participant(s);
- workplace supervisor;
- provider/application owner if the pilot contributes to recognised education design;
- authorised assessor where formal practical evidence is being tested;
- verifier/moderator where required;
- MouldMaster project contact;
- privacy/data contact;
- health and safety escalation contact.

A MouldMaster developer or app user must not be treated as an authorised workplace assessor unless the relevant provider/standards system separately authorises that role.

## Site consent checklist

Before any workplace activity:

- employer has explicitly agreed to the scope;
- learner participation is voluntary and documented through the appropriate provider/employer process;
- site health and safety rules take precedence over app content;
- machine operation remains under site authority;
- no app recommendation overrides approved process sheets, work instructions, permits or supervisor direction;
- practical tasks are limited to activities the learner is authorised and supervised to perform;
- emergency-stop and hazardous interventions are never created solely for the purpose of generating assessment evidence;
- confidential parts, customers and process settings are excluded unless separately authorised;
- allowed data fields and retention location are agreed;
- photography/audio/video rules are agreed before capture;
- permission to name the employer publicly is separate from permission to participate.

## Evidence design checklist

For every practical task under study, define:

- outcome or standard context;
- task description;
- workplace conditions;
- learner authority boundary;
- direct-observation requirement;
- acceptable evidence types;
- assessor/supervisor role;
- authenticity method;
- repetition/range required;
- safety stop criteria;
- escalation criteria;
- reassessment rule if applicable;
- retention period and access controls;
- whether evidence is formative-only or intended for provider-controlled summative use.

Simulation, screenshots, quiz passes and app completion must never be substituted for workplace evidence where direct practical demonstration is required.

## Data-minimisation rules

Prefer recording evidence metadata rather than raw operational data. A public evidence record may include:

- evidence ID;
- date range;
- generic machine/process category;
- task category;
- observation completed/not completed;
- assessor/supervisor role;
- outcome of review;
- issues found;
- remediation required;
- permission-controlled summary.

Do not put these in the public repository unless explicitly authorised and de-identified:

- learner names/contact details;
- customer names;
- proprietary part identifiers;
- raw machine traces;
- confidential process recipes;
- commercial scrap/cost data;
- incident details containing personal information;
- private photos, recordings or attachments.

## Pilot stop conditions

Pause or terminate the pilot when:

- participant safety may be compromised;
- activity exceeds learner authority;
- supervisor/assessor availability is inadequate;
- required consent is withdrawn;
- data is being captured beyond agreed scope;
- app wording is being interpreted as authority to alter a live process;
- confidentiality cannot be maintained;
- recognised-assessment claims are being made without provider/consent/moderation authority;
- a serious defect in learning content or assessment design is identified.

## Pilot outcomes to collect

A useful pilot should produce evidence about:

- learner comprehension/progression;
- usability/accessibility;
- alignment between digital learning and workplace practice;
- gaps in outcomes/content;
- assessor/supervisor workload;
- practicality of evidence capture;
- authenticity risks;
- privacy/data burden;
- safety/authority clarity;
- employer value;
- changes needed before wider use.

Do not convert a successful pilot into an NZQA approval claim. A pilot is evidence for later provider/NZQA decisions, not the decision itself.

## Readiness decision

A site may move from `prospective` to `pilot-ready` only when the applicable consent, role, safety, privacy and evidence-design fields above are completed and retained outside the public repository where sensitive details are involved.
