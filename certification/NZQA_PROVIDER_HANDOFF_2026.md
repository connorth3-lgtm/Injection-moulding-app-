# NZQA provider handoff pack — MouldMaster Academy

Status: **discussion/readiness pack only**. MouldMaster Academy is not represented here as NZQA approved, NZQA accredited, a registered provider, a qualification, or an organisation with consent to assess.

Checked against the public NZQA 2026 rule baseline on **2026-09-15**.

## Purpose of this handoff

Give an eligible NZQA-recognised provider enough structured information to decide whether it is worth progressing either:

1. a provider-owned **micro-credential** using MouldMaster content and evidence architecture; or
2. a **standards-based delivery/assessment** route using current Injection Moulding DASS standards within the provider's approved scope.

These routes are related but not interchangeable. A provider may choose one, both, or neither.

## What MouldMaster can bring to a provider discussion

Repository-controlled assets now include:
- a 46-chapter governed Injection Moulding Book from beginner through advanced diagnostics;
- technical source/evidence controls and chapter-level review states;
- ten draft learning outcomes suitable for provider review;
- exact chapter-to-outcome mappings;
- a current-vs-expired Injection Moulding standard map;
- formative knowledge checks, cases and simulations;
- draft practical/workplace evidence concepts;
- explicit safety, machine-authority, workplace and source boundaries;
- assessment/moderation field templates;
- source-freshness and fail-closed QA;
- privacy boundaries for public evidence records;
- ISO 9001/QMS support content relevant to evidence discipline, without certification claims.

The governed mapping lives in `../data/nzqa-education-readiness-v1.json` and its byte-identical learning-data mirror.

## Route A — micro-credential discussion

Current NZQA micro-credential approval content criteria require the provider/developer to establish:
- appropriate title and learning outcomes;
- evidence of need and stakeholder support;
- clear admission, credit/RPL, structure, assessment and completion requirements;
- an effective review process.

Provider accreditation additionally requires capability for appropriate delivery and fair, valid, consistent assessment/moderation.

### MouldMaster contribution
- draft title/purpose and learner groups;
- ten draft outcomes;
- curriculum coverage;
- assessment architecture;
- practical evidence concept;
- source and review governance;
- workload-study template;
- provider/industry consultation prompts.

### Provider decisions still required
- final title;
- NZQCF level;
- credit value based on defensible notional learning hours;
- admission/RPL/credit rules;
- final assessment and reassessment rules;
- delivery modes/sites;
- staff/assessor/moderator capability;
- learner support, accessibility, privacy, identity, complaints and appeals;
- record retention and official achievement reporting;
- review cycle and NZQA change-control responsibilities.

### Evidence still external
- formal needs analysis;
- written employer/learner/provider/industry support;
- relevant Industry Skills Board engagement where appropriate;
- pilot learner outcomes;
- assessor/moderator appointment and capability evidence;
- real workplace evidence arrangements.

### Important mapping limitation
MouldMaster chapter mappings indicate potentially relevant supporting knowledge/practice context only. They do **not** establish that all standard outcomes, evidence requirements, ranges, explanatory notes, practical conditions or CMR requirements are met.

## Draft learning outcomes for provider review

1. Explain the injection-moulding cycle, production purpose, hazards and safe operating boundaries.
2. Explain major machine functions and distinguish setpoints, actual process signals and machine limitations.
3. Explain how polymer structure, rheology, moisture, additives and thermal history affect process behaviour using grade-specific evidence where required.
4. Explain mould, feed, cooling, venting, ejection and hot-runner functions and identify tooling evidence needed before intervention.
5. Explain routine service and maintenance evidence, including pre-condition, intervention, post-condition and recovery verification.
6. Plan and interpret controlled process studies and process-window evidence without turning generic examples into production recipes.
7. Diagnose moulding defects using competing mechanisms, controlled evidence and safe escalation decisions.
8. Interpret basic measurement, capability, monitoring and quality-record evidence and document a defensible decision trail.
9. Explain advanced mould setup, verification and multi-cavity considerations while preserving machine-, mould- and site-specific authority boundaries.
10. Demonstrate, under authorised provider/workplace control, the ability to gather practical evidence and escalate conditions outside personal authority.

These outcomes are deliberately unlevelled until a provider completes NZQCF-level analysis.

## Assessment architecture offered for review

### Formative app lane
- question/retrieval practice;
- source interpretation;
- troubleshooting cases;
- simulated process studies;
- written reasoning and reflection.

### Provider-controlled summative lane
- approved instrument and version;
- authenticated learner evidence;
- marking schedule;
- assessor judgement;
- reassessment rules;
- moderation status;
- evidence retention;
- official result reporting.

### Workplace lane
- direct observation where required;
- authorised machine/mould/site context;
- practical evidence and supervisor/assessor verification;
- explicit escalation when activity exceeds learner authority.

A simulation, quiz result or app completion record must not be treated as workplace practical competence.

## Provider questions to resolve early

1. Which route is preferred: micro-credential, existing standards, programme component, non-formal training, or a staged combination?
2. Does the provider currently hold relevant consent to assess in the Injection Moulding domain/standards and levels?
3. Which CMR 13 personnel, resource and practical-access requirements must be demonstrated?
4. Who would own the MyNZQA application and ongoing approval/accreditation obligations?
5. What NZQCF level best fits the final outcomes?
6. What notional learning hours are defensible for the intended learner cohort?
7. What evidence must be observed in a real workplace rather than simulated?
8. Who can act as assessor, internal moderator and programme/credential owner?
9. What national external moderation obligations would apply to selected standards?
10. How will learner identity, enrolment, support, appeals, privacy, retention and recognised reporting be handled?
11. What employer/industry evidence is needed before the provider would support an application?
12. What pilot cohort and evaluation evidence should be collected before submission?

## Suggested provider review sequence

### Stage 1 — fit/no-fit
- review purpose, learner cohort and outcomes;
- decide likely route;
- screen for obvious level/scope problems;
- check provider eligibility and consent scope.

### Stage 2 — design
- agree title/outcomes;
- complete level analysis;
- complete workload study;
- decide standards, if any;
- design final assessment/workplace evidence;
- define support/admin/records.

### Stage 3 — quality assurance
- pre-moderate assessments;
- confirm assessor/moderator capability;
- confirm CMR/resource/practical access;
- confirm privacy/records/reassessment/appeals controls;
- set review/change governance.

### Stage 4 — evidence
- collect need/stakeholder support;
- conduct pilot/evaluation;
- close design and learner-support gaps;
- retain decision records.

### Stage 5 — application / formal delivery
Only after the provider confirms the applicable pathway should formal application or recognised standards-based assessment proceed.
