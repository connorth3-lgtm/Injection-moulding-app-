# Provider Partnership Outreach Pack — NZQA 2026

Status: draft outreach material. Adapt with the legal/business name and contact details before sending. Do not state or imply that MouldMaster Academy is NZQA approved or accredited.

## Outreach objective
Find an eligible NZQA-recognised tertiary education organisation (TEO) willing to evaluate MouldMaster Academy as the digital/technical foundation for a formally quality-assured injection-moulding micro-credential or another recognised training pathway.

Under the 2026 rules, the provider must be able to own the delivery/accreditation responsibilities. Relevant Industry Skills Board (ISB) engagement may also be appropriate for evidence of need and industry alignment, but an ISB does not replace an accredited provider.

## Provider qualification questions
Before treating a provider as a serious candidate, confirm:

- What NZQA provider category and current registration/accreditation status do you operate under?
- Does injection moulding/manufacturing fit your current scope, strategy and delivery capability?
- Have you previously developed, delivered or moderated NZQCF micro-credentials?
- Who would own the MyNZQA application and formal provider accountability?
- Who would own academic approval, assessment approval, moderation and learner records?
- What assessor/moderator competence evidence would you require for injection moulding?
- What workplace facilities, employer agreements or practical evidence arrangements would be required?
- Can the delivery model include self-paced digital learning plus controlled workplace/application assessment?
- What learner identity, enrolment, privacy, support, complaints and appeals systems would apply?
- How would official achievement reporting and award documents be handled?
- What evidence of industry need and stakeholder support would you expect before development begins?
- Should a relevant Industry Skills Board be engaged, and at what stage?
- What review period and change-control process would you expect?
- What commercial, IP and licensing model would you consider?
- What pilot evidence would you require before formal submission?

## Technical/academic scoping questions
These questions are for the provider/SME workshop, not claims to be fixed in advance:

- Which NZQCF level best fits the proposed outcomes using the current level descriptors?
- What is the defensible notional workload and therefore proposed credit value?
- Should the concept remain one micro-credential or be narrowed into a smaller first credential?
- Are any existing standards suitable and useful, or is outcome-based assessment more appropriate?
- Which outcomes require workplace/practical evidence rather than digital assessment?
- What evidence threshold is appropriate for safety-critical knowledge?
- How should reassessment work without weakening assessment validity?
- What moderation sample and frequency are appropriate to risk and cohort size?
- How will learner accessibility and support be evidenced for digital/workplace delivery?

- post-training supervisor feedback

## Learner/workforce needs interview prompts
Use consistent prompts so evidence can be aggregated:

- Which injection-moulding tasks or decisions cause the most avoidable rework, downtime or escalation?
- Where do new operators/setters most often lack underlying process understanding?
- Which troubleshooting habits distinguish competent staff from trial-and-error adjustment?
- Which safety or escalation decisions need stronger understanding?
- Which material/process concepts are hardest to learn on the job?
- Which practical evidence would convince you that a learner can apply the knowledge responsibly?
- What job roles should this training target first?
- What would make the training valuable enough for your organisation to release staff time for it?

Avoid collecting unnecessary personal information or commercially sensitive production data.
