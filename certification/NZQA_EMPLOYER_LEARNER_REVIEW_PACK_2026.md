# NZQA employer and learner review pack — 2026

Status: **consultation-ready template only**. It creates no evidence until real participants respond. It must not be used to imply NZQA approval, industry endorsement, workplace competence or provider accreditation.

## Purpose

Collect structured, permission-controlled evidence about whether MouldMaster's proposed learning outcomes, delivery approach and practical-evidence model address real learner and employer needs.

## Employer reviewer questions

Ask reviewers to comment only on areas they know directly.

1. Which injection-moulding capability gaps cause the most production, quality, safety, maintenance or training problems in your workplace?
2. Which roles need the strongest improvement: operator, setter, technician, maintenance/tooling, process engineer, quality, supervisor or another role?
3. Which of the proposed learning areas are essential before a learner is trusted with supervised practical work?
4. Which topics are useful knowledge but should **not** be treated as proof of practical competence?
5. What practical tasks require direct observation rather than simulation or written answers?
6. What evidence would you expect an assessor or supervisor to retain for those tasks?
7. What machine-, mould-, material- or site-specific boundaries must a generic digital resource avoid presenting as universal instructions?
8. What escalation decisions should a learner be expected to make when conditions exceed their authority or competence?
9. Which quality records, maintenance records or process records are realistic for learners to interpret without exposing confidential production data?
10. What would make the resource easier to use during workplace training?
11. Which outcomes appear unnecessary, missing, too advanced or too basic?
12. Would you be willing to review a revised outcome set or participate in a pilot? A yes answer records willingness only, not endorsement.

## Learner reviewer questions

Participation should be voluntary. Do not require disclosure of ethnicity, disability, health information or other sensitive personal data. If demographic perspectives are needed, obtain them through an approved provider/research process with appropriate privacy controls.

1. Which topics are hardest to learn in normal workplace training?
2. Which explanations or examples make injection moulding easier to understand?
3. Where does the resource assume too much prior knowledge?
4. Which parts feel too theoretical or disconnected from real work?
5. Are safety and authority limits clear enough?
6. Can you tell the difference between a learning exercise and an instruction to change a real machine or process?
7. Are diagrams, terminology, question wording and navigation understandable?
8. What support would help with reading, language, numeracy, accessibility or confidence without asking you to disclose personal medical information?
9. What feedback style helps you correct mistakes and keep learning?
10. What workplace practice would you still need after completing the digital material?

## Industry / community reviewer prompts

Where appropriate, ask organisations representing learners, whānau, Māori, Pacific peoples, disabled people, employers and sector communities to comment on:

- barriers to participation;
- language and cultural clarity;
- accessibility and digital access;
- workplace support expectations;
- whether examples reflect a useful range of workplaces and learners;
- whether consultation needs additional voices before formal programme design.

Do not treat one individual as representative of a whole community.

## Evidence record

For each review, record:

- evidence ID;
- date;
- organisation or reviewer category;
- reviewer role if voluntarily supplied;
- review method;
- material reviewed;
- questions answered;
- feedback summary;
- exact quotations only where permission is explicit;
- permission to name organisation/reviewer: yes/no/scope;
- permission to retain notes: yes/no;
- design changes proposed;
- design changes accepted/rejected with reason;
- unresolved questions;
- follow-up owner.

## Privacy boundary

Do not place learner names, contact details, disability/health information, proprietary part data, customer data, raw machine logs, confidential process settings or private attachments in the public repository. Store only de-identified summaries or evidence references that are safe and authorised for public retention.

## Gate rule

A completed review may contribute to the stakeholder-need/design evidence gate only when the feedback is real, attributable at an appropriate level, permission-controlled and retained. It does not by itself satisfy provider accreditation, consent to assess, moderation, workplace practical evidence or NZQA approval.
