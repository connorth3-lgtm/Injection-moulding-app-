# Black speck: observation to discriminating evidence

Status: Book-ready editorial artifact specification. Research/editorial only; not learner-runtime content.

## Teaching purpose

A black speck is an observation, not a root cause. The Book should teach learners to move from symptom description to discriminating evidence before changing process settings or replacing hardware.

## Figure composition

Draw a vertical decision ladder beginning with **Observed black speck / black dot / dark inclusion**. Split into four evidence pathways:

### Path A — foreign contamination

Look for evidence that the inclusion differs chemically or physically from the base polymer. Useful discriminators include optical examination, microscopy, SEM/EDS where appropriate, and polymer-identification methods such as FTIR or micro-IR.

Do not call contamination proven only because the defect is dark.

### Path B — degraded or carbonised polymer

Look for evidence of excessive thermal/residence history, stagnant regions, carbonised deposits, or degraded polymer chemistry. Check barrel/screw condition, dead spots, hot-runner stagnation, residence history, purge response and whether inclusions are chemically consistent with degraded base material.

Do not treat a temperature reduction as proof that degradation was the cause.

### Path C — feed/hopper/material carryover

Look for previous material, colourant, dusty regrind, hopper contamination, dryer/feed-system contamination, or incorrect cleanout sequence. Compare defect behaviour before and after controlled feed-system cleaning or material isolation.

Do not generalise hopper contamination from one plant case to every black-speck event.

### Path D — tooling / flow-path source

Look for evidence associated with filter meshes, runners, valve gates, seals, wear debris or local stagnation. Use location, recurrence pattern, intervention history and inspection evidence to distinguish a tooling/feed-path source from polymer degradation upstream.

Do not replace a mesh or runner component merely because one published case improved after doing so.

## Evidence ladder

Under the branches, show five escalating levels:

1. **Describe the defect** — location, size, frequency, cavity, colour, cycle/sample identity.
2. **Localise the source region** — machine/feed system, hot runner, mould, cavity or external contamination.
3. **Discriminate material identity** — microscopy/spectroscopy/elemental evidence where justified.
4. **Intervene on one supported hypothesis** — cleaning, isolation, component correction or process-history correction.
5. **Verify recovery over repeated production** — demonstrate sustained reduction, not one clean-looking part.

Add a prominent note:

> **A successful intervention strengthens a causal hypothesis only when the symptom, intervention and recovery are linked in time and competing explanations are controlled well enough to matter.**

## Case anchors

The 2023 applied production case reported black-spot share decreasing from **7.85% to 2.16%** after a filter-mesh design improvement, alongside Process Z increasing from **1.42 to 2.02**. Use this as a plant-specific intervention example, not as a universal first fix.

The 2014 black-dot thesis reported defect proportion decreasing from **0.65% to 0.34%**, a reported **47.69% reduction**, after a structured DMAIC workflow that included Gage R&R, cause/effect analysis, FMEA, Why-Why analysis, hypothesis testing, DOE and confirmation. The investigated factors included dirty/carbonised barrel and screw, previous material trapped in the barrel, raw-material degradation and hopper contamination.

The Book should contrast these two cases to show that similar visible symptoms can arise from different mechanisms and require different evidence paths.

## Learner exercise

### Scenario

Three lines produce visually similar black specks:

- **Line 1:** defects cluster after long interruptions and reduce after a controlled purge.
- **Line 2:** defects persist across material changes but drop after a feed-path component is corrected.
- **Line 3:** defects appear intermittently and microscopy shows inclusions chemically different from the base resin.

### Task

For each line, choose the next measurement or inspection that would most strongly discriminate among degradation, feed-path contamination, tooling/flow-path contamination and foreign-material contamination.

### Expected reasoning

Learners should choose evidence that tests a mechanism, not immediately prescribe temperature, purge compound, screw speed or mould changes. Strong answers explain what result would support or weaken each hypothesis.

## Editorial boundaries

- Black speck, black spot and black dot are symptom labels; mechanism must be established separately.
- Plant-specific intervention percentages remain attached to their cases.
- Do not promote filter-mesh redesign, barrel cleaning, hopper cleaning or temperature changes as universal first actions.
- The current evidence base still lacks a rights-clear shot-linked onset → intervention → verified-recovery dataset.
- Colour-changeover recovery evidence may teach chronology, but it is not black-speck evidence.
