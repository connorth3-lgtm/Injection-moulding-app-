# Black-spot interventions: what changed and what did not prove

Status: Book-ready case-table specification. Research/editorial only; not learner-runtime content.

## Teaching purpose

Show learners how two superficially similar black-dot/black-spot cases can support different conclusions, and why before/after improvement is not the same thing as a universal root-cause rule.

## Book table

| Case | Baseline | Investigation / intervention | Post-intervention | Sustainment evidence | What the case supports | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 applied black-spot case | Black-spot defect share **7.85%**; Process Z **1.42** | Why-Why analysis identified filter-mesh design as the study root cause; filter-mesh design was improved | Black-spot defect share **2.16%**; Process Z **2.02** | Work instruction and control plan | In that plant and process, the intervention was followed by a large measured reduction in black-spot defects | That filter-mesh design is a universal black-speck cause; that every dark inclusion should trigger the same hardware change; shot-by-shot causal chronology |
| 2014 black-dot DMAIC case | Black-dot defect proportion **0.65%** | Gage R&R, cause/effect, FMEA, Why-Why, hypothesis tests and DOE examined dirty/carbonised barrel and screw, previous material trapped in barrel, raw-material degradation and hopper contamination | Black-dot defect proportion **0.34%**; reported **47.69% reduction** | Work instruction, control plan and control charts | A structured measurement-and-hypothesis workflow can reduce defects without assuming one cause at the outset | That all four investigated factors caused every defect; that the same settings/interventions transfer to another machine/material; a public shot-order onset-to-recovery trace |

## Editorial callout

> **Improvement after intervention is evidence, but the strength of the causal claim depends on how well the symptom, hypothesis, intervention and recovery were linked and how competing explanations were handled.**

## Learner exercise

For each row, classify the following statements as **supported**, **plausible but unproven**, or **unsupported**:

1. “The intervention reduced the defect rate in the reported case.”
2. “The identified mechanism is the universal cause of black specks.”
3. “The same intervention should be the first action on another machine.”
4. “The process had a sustainment step after improvement.”
5. “A shot-by-shot recovery trajectory is publicly available.”

### Expected reasoning

A strong learner answer separates measured before/after outcome from mechanism generalisation. Both cases support that their respective structured interventions were followed by improved reported defect rates. Neither case establishes a universal first fix or supplies the desired rights-clear shot-linked onset → intervention → recovery dataset.
