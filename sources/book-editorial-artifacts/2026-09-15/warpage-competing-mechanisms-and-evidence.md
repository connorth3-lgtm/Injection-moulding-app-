# Warpage: competing mechanisms and evidence

Status: Book-ready cause/evidence-map specification. Research/editorial only; not learner-runtime content.

## Teaching purpose

Warpage can emerge from different physical pathways that produce similar final geometry. The Book should teach learners to discriminate among thermal imbalance, packing/pressure history, material morphology/shrinkage and fibre-orientation effects rather than using a one-variable troubleshooting rule.

## Figure composition

Place **Observed warpage** in the centre. Build four primary mechanism branches around it.

### Branch 1 — thermal imbalance

Possible evidence:

- unequal cooling-zone flow;
- unequal surface/tool temperature;
- different local cooling rates;
- cavity-to-cavity thermal asymmetry;
- warpage changing after verified cooling-balance correction.

Useful measurements: circuit flow, supply/return temperature, tool-surface temperature, cycle-resolved thermal data where available.

### Branch 2 — pressure / packing history

Possible evidence:

- cavity-pressure differences between regions or cavities;
- altered V/P transfer or packing history;
- gate-seal timing differences;
- local density/shrinkage response consistent with pressure history.

Useful measurements: cavity-pressure traces, gate-near pressure where available, part weight/dimensions, transfer timing.

### Branch 3 — morphology / shrinkage

Possible evidence:

- material- or conditioning-dependent dimensional change;
- melt/mould-temperature effects inconsistent with a simple hydraulic explanation;
- crystallinity or shrinkage behaviour relevant to the polymer family;
- time-after-moulding dimensional evolution.

Useful measurements: dimensional stability versus time, DSC or other material-characterisation evidence where justified, supplier-grade data.

### Branch 4 — fibre orientation / anisotropy

Possible evidence:

- spatial orientation aligned with flow history;
- directional shrinkage or stiffness;
- weld-line or flow-front features affecting local properties;
- XCT or validated orientation measurements/simulation where available.

Useful measurements: orientation imaging/XCT, directional mechanical response, flow-history comparison.

## Core teaching rule

> **Same shape does not mean same cause. Two parts can warp by a similar amount while requiring completely different next measurements and corrective actions.**

## Learner exercise

### Part A

Part A shows high warpage. Cooling-zone measurements reveal severe flow imbalance, while cavity-pressure traces are repeatable and symmetric.

### Part B

Part B shows similar final warpage. Cooling measurements are balanced, but cavity-pressure history differs strongly between the two sides of the part.

### Task

For each part, choose the next two measurements that would best strengthen or weaken the leading mechanism hypothesis.

### Expected reasoning

For Part A, learners should prioritise thermal verification and post-balance geometry response before changing packing settings.

For Part B, learners should investigate pressure/packing history, gate behaviour and local shrinkage response before assuming a cooling problem.

A strong answer also keeps morphology and fibre orientation available as competing hypotheses when the first mechanism does not explain the evidence.

## Editorial boundaries

- Avoid “increase/decrease mould temperature” or “increase packing pressure” as generic warpage recipes.
- A statistical association between a process setting and warpage does not by itself establish the physical mechanism.
- Simulation may illustrate orientation or thermal fields only when clearly labelled and physically validated where possible.
- Material family and grade remain essential context for shrinkage/morphology claims.
