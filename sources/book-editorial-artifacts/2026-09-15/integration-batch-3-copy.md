# Integration batch 3 — exercises and case-callout copy

This editorial handoff supplies compact copy for case/exercise surfaces that should sit beside the enriched chapter prose. It is not learner-runtime content.

## Black-spot intervention comparison

Two reported production cases improved black-spot/black-dot performance through different investigation paths. One reported defect share moving from 7.85% to 2.16% after a filter-mesh design improvement. Another reported 0.65% to 0.34% after a DMAIC programme that tested several contamination/degradation hypotheses. Use the pair to ask learners what each case supports—and what it cannot prove without a shot-linked chronology.

## Distributed melt-control outcomes

Group the older dynamic-melt-control studies under one research-family label. Reported outcomes include Cp increasing from 0.52 to 1.5 in one multi-cavity study, 50–400% Cp improvement across eleven measured dimensions in a commercial application, and lower part-weight sensitivity/variation in a self-regulating-valve study. The teaching point is that local melt control can affect quality; the numbers are not transferable controller-performance targets.

## Eight-cavity cooling worksheet

Use the source-backed system values—0.6 to 2.0 L/min total flow and 416 to 362 kPa pressure loss after cleaning—then give learners an explicitly synthetic cavity-by-cavity balance table. Ask whether system recovery proves local balance. The expected answer is no: local flow, thermal response and part quality still need to be checked by cavity.

## Minimum maintenance-record repair exercise

Start with: “Mould 7 — parts out of tolerance. Tool cleaned and repaired. Running OK.” Ask learners to add the fields needed for future diagnosis: stable component identity, production exposure, measured pre-condition, specific intervention, measured post-condition, comparable part-quality verification and the observation period used to call the process recovered.

## Boundary

Case values must stay attached to their sources. Synthetic values must remain labelled synthetic. Exercises should reward evidence selection and causal discrimination, not memorisation of a parameter change.
